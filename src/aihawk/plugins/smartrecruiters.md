The job page shows the posting and an "I'm interested" link, `#st-apply`. Click it: the form opens on an oneclick-ui page under the heading "Easy apply". A cookie banner (`#onetrust-reject-all-handler`, "Reject all") may cover the page: reject it first when it is there.

The form is inside a shadow root, and it is in steps. The first step, "Personal information", holds the name, email, City and phone fields, then Experience, Education, Your Profiles, Resume and "Message to the Hiring Team". Its footer "Next" button goes to the next step, which may ask "Preliminary questions". Fill what the step shows, go on with "Next", and read the page again. Report ready only on the last step, the one whose footer control sends the form.

Name and email: type each with browser_type, using the selector that browser_snapshot gives for it verbatim, for example `#first-name-input >> #first-name-input`, `#email-input >> #email-input`, `#confirm-email-input >> #confirm-email-input`.

Phone, in two parts. First the country: the combobox labelled "Country code" shows a flag and a dial code, which differs per posting (+1, +971). If it is already the number's country, leave it. Click it with browser_click_at, at the point browser_snapshot gives in its `at`. A list opens with a search field, "Search by country/region or code": type the country name into it with browser_type (`input[placeholder='Search by country/region or code']`), for example "United Kingdom". Choose its row as described under Menus. The button then shows the dial code, "+44". Then the number: type into the field labelled "Phone number", with browser_type and its snapshot selector (`#spl-form-element_5 >> #spl-form-element_5 >> #spl-form-element_5`), the national number without the dial code and without a leading 0, written as `<national number>` here. The page shows it in groups and keeps "+44". Do not type the dial code into the number field. Read the field under the phone: if it says "Please provide a valid phone number", the page refused the number. Numbers in the range 07700 900000 to 07700 900999 are refused this way: the page turns them into "1481 7700900123" (Guernsey). Then report the form as blocked and say so; do not try other spellings of that number.

Upload the CV with browser_upload_files and the selector `[data-test='resume-upload'] >> div`: the dropzone "Choose a file or drop it here" in the Resume section. Do not use the file input `#spl-dropzone-file-input-1` or the label that points at it: the input covers the dropzone, so a click on the label lands on the input and fails. Leave the input inside `apply-with-resume-container` alone. Confirm the attachment with a screenshot: the file name (test-application.pdf) appears under the dropzone with a trash icon. The page's text does not show the name, and the tool may say the input is empty again: the page clears the input once it has read the file, and that is normal. Do not upload again when the name shows.

Menus here are the shadow-root case of the menus rules: the country list, the City suggestions and a dropdown's answers all open under their field, inside the shadow root, and browser_snapshot does not list their rows. The row to click is the innermost element whose text starts with the row's text and whose box has a width: walk the shadow roots with browser_evaluate to find it. A choice has taken when the closed field shows it, for example the country button showing "+44", and the error under the field is gone.

The footer "Next" button has no selector in browser_snapshot: read its centre the same way (browser_evaluate reads, then browser_click_at). Leave "Add" under Experience and Education alone unless the page requires an entry: "Please provide at least one work experience entry" under Experience means it does. Then add one from the CV: Title, Company and Office location suggest as you type, and From and To take MM/YYYY.

The form scrolls inside its own container, so PageUp and PageDown move nothing: seen on a real form, 40 steps lost pressing them. To reach a field, call its `scrollIntoView()` with browser_evaluate, walking the shadow roots.

The "Preliminary questions" step often asks for the right to work, sponsorship, and a block of required boxes with the person's name and today's date, stating that the answers are true. Its fields carry an id `question_<uuid>` on a wrapper and on the control alike: type into `input#question_<uuid>` or `textarea#question_<uuid>`, never the bare id, which browser_type refuses ("not an input"). Seen on a real form, one step lost that way.

The last step's footer control sends the application: `[data-test='footer-submit']`, labelled "Submit". Its label is what the page says, for example "Submit application". Never click it. Name it as the submit control only when a plain CSS selector matches that control alone.

After sending: read the page: if the form is still there, the application was not received.
